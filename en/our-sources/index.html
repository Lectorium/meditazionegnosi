SOURCES AND REFERENCE TEXTS

Our sources

Even before turning to written texts, the Gnostic search begins with an inner need. It is a profound call, an intuition that leads human beings to question their origin and the meaning of their presence in the world.

In this perspective, true knowledge comes not only from what we read, but above all from what we discover within. Texts then become tools, signposts, symbols: not dogmas, but maps that guide the journey.

Gnosis does not arise from this world, but from Spirit’s longing for its origin. Written sources are therefore not the starting point; they support a knowledge that remains, first and foremost, an inner experience.

Principal reference texts

Among the most significant texts for this path are:

  • The Gospel of John
  • The Gospel of Thomas
  • The Gospel of Philip
  • The Gospel of Truth
  • The Book of Ecclesiastes (Qoheleth)
  • The Hymn of the Pearl
  • The Acts of the Apostles (read symbolically)
  • The Interrogatio Johannis
  • The Book of the Two Principles

Other sources

Gnostic inquiry also draws inspiration from:

  • ancient wisdom writings
  • Christian mystical traditions
  • symbolic literature
  • philosophies of inner knowledge

We do not place every tradition on the same level. Even in very different texts, we seek traces of a fundamental rupture: the distance between the good Father and the material world, between Spirit and the powers that hold it captive. Not everything called “spiritual” leads to liberation.

Our approach

These sources are offered as tools for study and meditation. We do not read these texts to build a comforting spirituality. We question them to recognize our condition, distinguish voices that speak of the Light from those that justify this world, and seek a knowledge that becomes liberation.
