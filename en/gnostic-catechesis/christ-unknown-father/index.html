GNOSTIC CATECHESIS · CHAPTER 6

Christ, Messenger of the Unknown Father

Italiano · Français · English

Which Christ?

On our path, Christ is neither the representative of religious power nor the guarantor of the material order. He is the messenger of the good Father. This distinction changes how we read his words: his message does not invite us to accept suffering as the divine will, but to recognize a reality that does not belong to the laws of this world.

The Father unknown to the world

The Father is not a jealous sovereign demanding obedience through fear. He is the principle of Light and spiritual life. In this dualist perspective, the Gospel language of the Father and the Kingdom can be read as a call towards a homeland untouched by violence and corruption. We do not claim this is the only Christian interpretation: it is the one guiding our catechesis.

A Kingdom not of this world

In the Gospel of John, Jesus declares that his Kingdom is not of this world (18:36). For us, this is not merely an invitation to withdraw from politics: it expresses a difference in nature. The Father’s Kingdom is not an improved version of earthly kingdoms. It is founded neither on conquest nor on property, fear, or death.

Knowledge that awakens

Christ does not offer only a set of commandments. He invites us to recognize what we are and where we come from. In the reading we propose, the Gospel of Thomas emphasizes seeking and self-knowledge. This knowledge is neither the accumulation of information nor intellectual superiority: it is the awakening of a spiritual memory that material life tends to obscure.

The problem of the cross

We do not interpret Christ’s suffering as a bloody payment demanded by the Father. A perfectly good God would not need the torture of an innocent person in order to grant mercy. Rather, the cross reveals how far the world can go in rejecting a message that challenges its laws. Gnostic traditions have expressed the relationship between Christ, the body, and the Passion in different ways: we do not reduce them to a single formula.

Christ and power

Whenever a spiritual message is used to justify violence, persecution, or domination, a contradiction becomes apparent. The Father does not impose himself by the sword. The history of religious institutions is not automatically identical with the history of Christ’s message. That is why we return to the texts, but above all to inner discernment.

The good Father and vulnerable beings

A message that comes from Good cannot authorize cruelty towards the defenceless. Compassion for animals, care for those who suffer, and rejection of avoidable violence are not marginal details. They are our attempt to live, even now, according to a principle different from predation.

Do not worship an image

The mind often seeks a visible figure to possess and defend. But no image can contain the unknown Father. Even language about Christ can become an idol if we merely repeat formulas without understanding them. Faithfulness to the message does not consist in multiplying declarations of belonging, but in allowing it to transform the way we see.

The proclamation of liberation

Christ reminds us that we are not destined to identify forever with what is born and dies. The call of the Homeland of Light does not erase present suffering, but prevents us from treating it as the ultimate truth. The Father does not ask us to love our chains; he calls us to recognize them and not impose them on others.

Listen without blindly imitating

In silence, reread a Gospel saying that has struck you. Ask whether it leads you towards greater clarity and compassion, or merely makes you feel part of a superior group. The next chapter will explore Gnosis as awakening: the difference between believing that we know and beginning truly to know.

Sources and further reading
