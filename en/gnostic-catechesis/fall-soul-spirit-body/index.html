GNOSTIC CATECHESIS · CHAPTER 4

The Fall: Soul, Spirit, and Body

Italiano · Français · English

Where do we come from?

Having distinguished the good Father from the material creator, the question becomes unavoidable: why are we here? If Spirit belongs to the Homeland of Light, how can it be caught up in a world ruled by time and death? From the standpoint of radical dualism, we do not answer by claiming that the Father sent us to earth to test us. Good does not need to build a prison in order to teach freedom. Our condition results from separation, not from an educational plan devised by the Father.

The fall is not original guilt

Speaking of a fall does not mean accusing every human being of having freely chosen Evil. In absolute dualism, the opposing principle is independent of Good: its action does not arise from some hidden fault in the Father or his creatures. Accounts of the fall express, through different images, the tragedy of a spiritual reality caught up in an order to which it does not belong. We need not turn these accounts into literal chronicles to understand their meaning.

What we mean by the body

The body is our instrument of experience in the world, but also the seat of hunger, illness, desire, and death. We can care for it without mistaking it for our ultimate identity. Despising or harming it does not bring us closer to the Father: it would merely add suffering to what we already endure. Spiritual discipline means, rather, not allowing every bodily need to become our master.

Soul and the movements of the psyche

We call Soul the dimension that experiences emotions, memories, fears, attachments, and the continuity of personal experience. It is involved in earthly life, receives its impressions, and can identify with them. When we say “I”, we often mean precisely this interweaving of memories and desires. Inner observation reveals how changeable it is: today we love what we rejected yesterday; today we fear what tomorrow we may forget.

Spirit and the memory of Light

On this path, Spirit designates what points towards the Father and cannot be explained solely by a person’s psychological history. It is not an intense emotion, a spectacular vision, or an especially elevated thought. It is the possibility of recognizing an origin that does not belong to matter. This distinction is essential: not every inner voice is spiritual, and not every longing for transcendence is already Gnosis.

The illusion of identification

The world teaches us to identify with our name, profession, body, opinions, and possessions. When something threatens these identifications, we react as though our very existence were threatened. Yet all these things change. The work of breaking conditioning is to see our attachments without pretending they do not exist. Merely declaring oneself Spirit is not enough: we must patiently recognize everything that holds us captive.

Compassion for those who have fallen

If we share a condition of captivity, we have no reason to despise other living beings. Animals are not guilty of the laws that constrain them; human beings cannot be reduced to their mistakes. This does not erase responsibility for actions, but it changes our outlook. Liberation is not born of ostentatious superiority: it is born of clarity and compassion.

The fall in texts and traditions

Gnostic and dualist accounts of the fall are not all the same. Some describe Light mingling with darkness; others speak of spirits confined in bodies; still others describe separation from an original condition. This catechesis does not claim these narratives agree in every detail. It draws out their spiritual core according to radical dualism: the Father did not create the captivity, and Spirit’s homeland is not here.

An exercise in discernment

In silence, observe a desire that frequently returns. Ask where it comes from, what it promises, and how long the satisfaction it offers lasts. Then observe a fear: does it concern your deepest reality, or something you may lose over time? Do not force an answer. This exercise is not intended to deny emotions, but to stop confusing them with the whole of our identity.

Towards the cycle of existences

If the body dies and spiritual existence is not identical with it, what happens to consciousness caught up in matter? The next chapter will examine reincarnation and repetition, distinguishing the convictions of our path from the different historical formulations found in Gnostic traditions.
