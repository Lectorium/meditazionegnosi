GNOSTIC CATECHESIS · CHAPTER 10

The Return to the Homeland of Light

Longing for another homeland

There is a form of longing that has nothing to do with a place once visited or a time long past. It is the feeling that no possession, victory, or material security can permanently fill what is missing. In our catechesis, this longing becomes the language of a spiritual origin: the Homeland of Light.

The Father’s Kingdom is not a material paradise

We do not imagine liberation as a richer and more pleasant version of earthly life. A paradise built around the same needs, bodies, and attachments would still be governed by the laws of the Demiurge. The Homeland of Light belongs to a different nature, beyond corruption and death.

Returning is not conquest

Spirit does not need to seize the world to become free. It is not liberated by dominating others, accumulating power, or proving superiority. The path instead requires discernment, detachment, and openness to knowledge. It is neither a competition nor an undertaking in which the ego can proclaim itself victorious.

What does salvation mean?

From the perspective of radical dualism, salvation is liberation from captivity, not absolution for a fault that the Father imposed upon his creatures. Good did not create Evil and then demand to be thanked when it saves us. Returning to the Light expresses the overcoming of separation and recognition of spiritual origin.

Hope and mystery

No one can describe with empirical certainty what lies beyond death. Traditions have used images of return, reunion, and liberation. We can receive their spiritual meaning without turning them into verified accounts. Our conviction about dualism does not require us to invent details of the afterlife.

Death is not a shortcut

It bears repeating: liberation does not automatically coincide with the death of the body. Seeking to hasten or bring about death would confuse a law of the material world with returning to the Father. Present life remains the place where we can cultivate knowledge, compassion, and responsibility towards other living beings.

The destiny of Evil

Radical dualism recognizes two primordial and distinct principles. We must therefore avoid imagining that Evil becomes Good or that the Father was secretly its cause. Our horizon is the liberation of what belongs to Light from a dominion alien to it. Traditions have portrayed the completion of this separation in different ways.

Compassion to the last step

We cannot long to return to the Light while remaining indifferent to the suffering of creatures who share our condition. The spiritual path is not an escape into selfishness. If suffering is real, every act that reduces it matters. Nonviolence, simplicity, and care for the defenceless are everyday reflections of the direction we have chosen.

Knowledge without fear

A catechesis should not keep people bound through threats of invisible punishments. The good Father has no need of terror. We can speak seriously about captivity without claiming to know the individual destiny of those who do not share our vision. Genuine inquiry requires inner freedom.

The path continues

We began by questioning conditioning, distinguished the good Father from the Demiurge, and reflected on the fall, the repetition of existences, Christ’s message, Gnosis, the Consolamentum, and spiritual discipline. These chapters are not a destination. They invite us to examine each day whether what we have understood changes how we live. Times change, but Spirit does not.

Sources and further reading

This is a contemporary reading of radical dualism; historical Gnostic and Cathar traditions did not all share the same teachings.
