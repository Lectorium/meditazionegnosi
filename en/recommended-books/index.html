MEDITAZIONE E GNOSI

Recommended books

Italiano · Français · English

This is not a library of interchangeable spiritualities. These works examine the origin of Evil, the captivity of Spirit and the return to the Homeland of Light. Our doctrinal standpoint is absolute dualism: the good Father is alien to the material world of corruption and death. Not all these authors share that conviction; their differences must be respected.

Gnostic and Cathar sources

Book of the Two Principles · Anonymous Cathar author, 13th century

The text closest to our catechesis: Good and Evil as distinct, primordial principles, without making the Father the cause of Evil.

Interrogatio Johannis (Secret Supper) · Medieval apocryphal text

A dualist account of the world’s origin, Satan’s actions and the condition of souls in matter.

Gospel of Thomas · Sayings attributed to Jesus

The search, self-knowledge and the Kingdom beyond appearances: essential reading for our path.

Gospel of Philip · Ancient Gnostic text

Symbols, spiritual union and awakening: language not to be reduced to literalism.

Gospel of Truth · Ancient Gnostic Christian text

Ignorance and forgetfulness mark the human condition; knowledge of the Father opens the way home.

Hymn of the Pearl · Acts of Thomas

A prince forgets his origin and receives a call to remember: a striking image of awakening.

Apocryphon of John · Ancient Gnostic text

An elaborate account of the Demiurge and the powers of the lower world, to be compared with radical dualism.

Philosophy, birth and suffering

The Trouble with Being Born · Emil Cioran

An uncompromising work on the tragedy of birth. Not a Gnostic catechism, but a critique of celebrating material life.

The New Gods / The Evil Demiurge · Emil Cioran

A meditation on the creator and evil that directly engages the Gnostic problem of the world.

The Temptation to Exist · Emil Cioran

A disenchanted reflection on identity, desire and the world’s illusory promises.

The World as Will and Representation · Arthur Schopenhauer

Blind will and the structural suffering of existence provide a powerful comparison, distinct from our two-principle doctrine.

History and studies of Gnosis

Libertà va cercando. Il catarismo nella Commedia di Dante · Maria Soresina

An original reading of Dante’s Divine Comedy through Cathar spirituality. Maria Soresina traces references to the Good Christians and the consolamentum. I had the pleasure of giving lectures with her and warmly remember both her generosity and her research.

Moretti & Vitali, 2009

The Cathar Religion · Jean Duvernoy

A historical reference for distinguishing medieval evidence from modern reinterpretations.

Lo gnosticismo · Hans Jonas

A classic on humanity’s estrangement from the cosmos, the Demiurge and liberating knowledge. Best read alongside ancient sources.

Il Dio alieno · Hans Jonas

A modern study of estrangement from the world and the call of a transcendent origin.

La gnosi e lo spirito della tarda antichità · Hans Jonas

A demanding study of the Gnostic view of the cosmos and existence.

The Gnostic Gospels · Elaine Pagels

A comparison of Gnostic texts and early Christianity, attentive to the diversity of communities.

Further reading

The Fall into Time · Emil Cioran

A reflection on time and the loss of an original condition, evocative of the theme of the fall.

Novels, science fiction and prison-worlds

Ubik · Philip K. Dick

Reality unravels and time falters: who is alive, who is captive, and what governs appearances?

VALIS · Philip K. Dick

A revelation questions the nature of the cosmos and its creator, between fiction, theology and Gnosis.

The Three Stigmata of Palmer Eldritch · Philip K. Dick

Artificial worlds and manipulated perceptions suggest consciousness trapped beneath a deceptive power.

The Man in the High Castle · Philip K. Dick

History and reality appear less stable than we think: an invitation to question the imposed order.

Neuromancer · William Gibson

Identity and consciousness move through a controlled digital universe: a technological image of captivity.

The City and the Stars · Arthur C. Clarke

An enclosed city, administered collective memory and the desire to cross the boundaries of the known world.

We · Yevgeny Zamyatin

A perfectly regulated society suppresses individuality; awakening begins when its order ceases to seem natural.

Nineteen Eighty-Four · George Orwell

Power seeks to rule truth and memory. Not a Gnostic novel, but a picture of deception becoming an entire world.

Brave New World · Aldous Huxley

Captivity can arise through pleasure, consumption and conditioning, without visible chains.

His Dark Materials · Philip Pullman

Cosmic authority, rebellion and freedom of conscience shape a story with Gnostic resonances.

This selection will grow. Not every book is historically Gnostic: some invite comparison without sharing our doctrine.
