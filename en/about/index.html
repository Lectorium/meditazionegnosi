About me

Simone Pozzi · In search of the Homeland of Light

Spiritual inquiry has been part of my life since early childhood. My first memories of spiritual experience date back to the age of five or six. I began meditating as a child through martial arts, later exploring subtle energies and healing practices.

An old book in my family’s library, Detti segreti di Gesù, edited by Luigi Moraldi, left a lasting impression on me. A passage comparing the world to a corpse sharpened questions I had long carried: what is this world, where do we come from, and who are we?

Around the age of thirteen I began studying esoteric traditions. I became vegetarian at twenty-three. From eighteen onwards, I visited monasteries, hermitages, temples, and ashrams belonging to different traditions.

At twenty-seven I left my job as a team supervisor to spend several months in a hermitage in the hills near Verona. I subsequently spent about a year at a Benedictine monastery open to interreligious dialogue, undertook a period of seminary training, and stayed at an ashram in India.

I was looking less for a religion to join than for answers to questions I had carried since childhood. In Gnosis, and especially in radical dualism, I found an answer to questions that had accompanied me for years: the good Father is not the creator of this world, matter is not our homeland, and Spirit does not belong here. I was not looking for a spirituality that would make this existence more comfortable, but for knowledge that would expose our captivity and the possibility of liberation.

In 2009 I founded the Gnostic association Chiesa Catara to pursue spiritual study and outreach. Catharism remains an important part of my journey, though I do not wish to confine my search to a single tradition.
