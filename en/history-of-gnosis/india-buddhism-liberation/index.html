MEDITAZIONE E GNOSI

India, Buddhism, and Paths to Liberation

The world and illusion

India’s philosophical and religious traditions developed profound reflections on ignorance, the cycle of rebirth, and liberation. Some Hindu schools distinguish a person’s deepest identity from temporary identifications. These themes allow comparison with the Gnostic quest without assuming direct descent.

The Buddha and attachment

In Buddhism, the fundamental problem is not an evil creator but the ignorance that fuels desire, attachment, and suffering. Awakening means seeing the condition of existence and following a path to liberation. An important difference from many Gnostic schools is that the Buddha does not base his teaching on a divine spark imprisoned by a Demiurge.

The value of comparison

Comparison must not erase the decisive difference. In our radical dualism, suffering does not arise merely from mistaken perception or desire: it belongs to the very structure of the material world, which does not proceed from the good Father. Buddhism offers a different answer and must be understood on its own terms, not retrospectively turned into a doctrine of the Demiurge.
