MEDITAZIONE E GNOSI

Zoroaster and the Conflict Between Light and Darkness

Choosing between two paths

In Zoroastrian tradition, the conflict between truth and falsehood, order and destruction takes on both cosmic and moral dimensions. Ahura Mazda represents wisdom and goodness, while Angra Mainyu is associated with destructive power. This is not yet the cosmology of Christian Gnostic schools, but it introduces a recurring question: is evil merely an imperfection, or a force opposed to goodness?

Human responsibility

The conflict does not concern the heavens alone. Human beings participate through their thoughts, words, and actions. Light is therefore more than a poetic image: it becomes a principle guiding life. This ethical dimension distinguishes Persian tradition from later accounts of the Demiurge, while revealing a shared concern with the problem of evil.

A starting point

Tracing the roots of Gnosis does not mean calling Zoroaster a “Gnostic”. It means recognizing that the ancient world had already developed powerful ways of speaking about light, darkness, and liberation.
