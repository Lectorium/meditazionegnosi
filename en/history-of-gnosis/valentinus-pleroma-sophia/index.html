MEDITAZIONE E GNOSI

Valentinus and Divine Fullness

A different Christianity

Valentinus was one of the major teachers of the second century. His school developed a reading of Christianity in which Christ’s message was connected with knowledge of humanity’s spiritual origins. Valentinian communities produced varied texts and interpretations rather than one unchanging catechism.

The Pleroma and Sophia

The Pleroma signifies divine fullness, described through the Aeons. In the story of Sophia, Wisdom, a rupture gives rise to lack and ignorance. The myth is not merely a tale of heavenly beings: it also speaks of separation from fullness and the longing to recover it.

Returning through knowledge

In Valentinian tradition, salvation is closely linked to the revelation brought by Christ. Knowing means understanding where one comes from and the reality towards which one is called. This perspective makes Valentinian Christianity especially important in the history of Gnosis. It must not, however, be confused with the absolute dualism of our catechesis: in Valentinian cosmologies, Sophia’s drama and the origin of ignorance do not amount to two eternal, independent, irreconcilable principles. A shared language of liberation does not erase this difference.
