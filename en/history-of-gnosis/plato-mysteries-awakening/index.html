MEDITAZIONE E GNOSI

Plato, the Mysteries, and Awakening

Shadow and reality

In Plato’s famous allegory of the cave, people mistake shadows for the whole of reality. Knowledge requires a difficult movement: turning around, emerging, and learning to see. The parallel with Gnostic awakening is suggestive, though the two philosophies are not identical. Plato does not simply teach that the world was made by an evil power.

Initiation into the Mysteries

Ancient mystery cults used rituals, symbols, and initiatory journeys to represent a passage from one condition to another. We do not know every detail of their practices, which were often reserved for initiates, but inner transformation was an important theme.

Knowledge is not enough

What connects these settings is the idea that seeing truth requires a change in the human being. Knowledge is not merely possessing information: it requires distinguishing appearance from truth. From our radical dualist standpoint, this discernment does not mean reconciling ourselves with the material world, but recognizing its limits and seeking what lies beyond its order.
