MEDITAZIONE E GNOSI

The Bogomils

The good God, the creator, and dissident Christianity in the Balkans

IT · FR · EN

A faith born in the Balkans

Bogomilism emerged in medieval Bulgaria amid social, political, and religious tensions. Tradition associates the movement with a priest named Bogomil, mentioned in hostile sources. Teachings questioning ecclesiastical authority and outward forms of worship spread from this setting. Bogomil communities should not be imagined as identical copies of ancient Gnostic schools: they belonged to another age, but forcefully revisited the relationship between the good God and the material world.

The creator of the visible world

In accounts of Bogomil teaching, Satan or Satanael plays a central role in shaping and ruling the material world. Visible reality is not presented as a transparent expression of divine goodness. Above it stands the heavenly Father, while the human soul retains a connection to a higher origin. Versions of the narrative vary, but their spiritual meaning remains recognizable: what rules the world is not necessarily the highest good.

Christ and liberation

Christ’s message is understood as a call to escape the dominion of the lower world and recognize the true Father. Faith is not exhausted by institutional membership or participation in solemn ceremonies. Inner transformation, prayer, and a simple way of life matter more than outward displays of religious power.

A critique of ecclesiastical wealth

Bogomil criticism of clergy, property, and religious authority was not merely a social protest. It also arose from a spiritual outlook in which wealth and domination could bind people to the very world from which they sought liberation. Simplicity in communal life therefore had religious significance. This is one reason Bogomilism matters to the history of medieval Christian dissent.

A complex legacy

The spread of dualist ideas across the Balkans and their possible relationships with Western movements require attention to differences between communities. We need not imagine a single, unbroken line of descent to recognize recurring questions about evil, the soul’s origin, inner freedom, and the distance between spiritual teaching and power. These themes make the Bogomils an essential part of this historical journey.
