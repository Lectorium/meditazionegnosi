MEDITAZIONE E GNOSI

Alexander, Alexandria, and Hermes

The meeting of cultures

Alexander the Great’s conquests encouraged extensive contact between peoples, languages, and traditions. In the Hellenistic world, Alexandria in Egypt became a centre where Greek, Egyptian, and Eastern forms of knowledge could meet. Rather than imagining a single secret school, we should understand an environment rich in exchange.

Hermetic wisdom

Texts attributed to Hermes Trismegistus explore the relationship between the divine, the cosmos, and humanity. Self-knowledge and spiritual rebirth are central themes. Hermeticism is not identical to Gnosis, nor to our radical dualism. Some Hermetic writings assign a positive value to the cosmos: a perspective incompatible with our distinction between the good Father and the material order. Comparison remains useful for exploring human origins, but must not erase this opposition.

The ground from which Gnosis emerged

When Gnostic teachers appeared in the first centuries of the Common Era, many questions were already circulating around the Mediterranean: what is real, where does the soul come from, and how can transformative knowledge be attained? Alexandria exemplifies this meeting of possibilities.
